# BMM Validation Criteria

Use the following criteria to validate a BMM goal model against the supplied source material and the stated modelling purpose.

Validation concerns whether the model adequately represents the relevant content of an exercise, scenario, specification, interview, policy document, stakeholder statement, or other source. It examines alignment between the model and external material rather than merely the model’s internal construction.

## Validation scope

Before assessing alignment, determine:

* which material constitutes the source;
* what the model is intended to represent;
* which part of the source falls within the intended modelling scope;
* whether the source contains explicit modelling requirements;
* whether some source material is contextual and not expected to appear in the model.

Do not assume that every sentence in the source must be represented. Assess completeness in relation to the modelling purpose and any explicit requirements.

If the modelling purpose or scope is unclear, identify this as a limitation affecting the validation.

## Traceability from source to model

For each source statement that is relevant to the modelling purpose, examine whether:

* it has a corresponding model element or relationship;
* the corresponding model content can be identified unambiguously;
* all important parts of the source statement have been represented;
* the model preserves the meaning of the source statement;
* several distinct source statements have been combined in a way that changes or obscures their meaning;
* the source statement has been represented more than once without a clear reason.

When identifying a correspondence, quote or reference both:

1. the relevant source passage; and
2. the corresponding model element or relationship.

Do not claim that a source statement is represented merely because the source and model use similar words. Examine whether their meanings actually correspond.

## Traceability from model to source

For each model element and relationship, examine whether:

* it is directly supported by the source;
* it is a reasonable interpretation of the source;
* it depends on an additional assumption;
* it extends the source with unsupported content;
* it contradicts the source;
* no identifiable source basis can be found.

A model element does not need to repeat the exact wording of the source. Reformulation is acceptable when the original meaning is preserved.

Distinguish clearly between an unsupported addition and an explicit assumption. An assumption extends the source but may be acceptable when assumptions are permitted and clearly stated. An unsupported addition is presented as though it came from the source even though no basis for it can be identified.

## Coverage and completeness

Examine whether the model includes:

* all source content explicitly required by the task;
* the principal goals expressed or implied by the source;
* measurable targets stated in the source;
* actions, approaches, capabilities, or resources relevant as means;
* factual circumstances relevant as influencers;
* important relationships among these elements;
* assumptions required to understand how the model represents the source.

Identify source content that is relevant to the modelling purpose but missing from the model.

Do not treat irrelevant contextual details as omissions. Explain why an omitted source statement is relevant before identifying its absence as a validation weakness.

## Faithfulness of goals

For each goal, examine whether:

* it corresponds to a desired state expressed or reasonably implied by the source;
* the relevant actor, object, quality, or outcome has been preserved;
* the model has not weakened or strengthened the desired state without support;
* distinctions between different desired states have been preserved;
* a source statement describing an existing fact has not been transformed into a goal without justification;
* a goal introduced through an assumption is clearly distinguished from a source-derived goal.

Pay particular attention to changes in meaning caused by replacing terms such as:

* “all” with “some”;
* “several times a day” with “regularly”;
* “ensure” with “encourage”;
* “relevant” with “accurate”;
* “available” with “installable.”

Such formulations may appear similar while expressing different desired states.

## Faithfulness of objectives

For each objective, examine whether:

* its target corresponds to a target stated or supported by the source;
* numerical values, units, populations, baselines, and deadlines have been preserved correctly;
* an objective has been introduced even though the source provides no basis for its target;
* a qualitative source statement has been converted into a quantitative target through an unstated assumption;
* the objective measures the source-derived goal rather than a different property;
* the objective narrows or expands the intended scope without support.

When the student has introduced a numerical target not found in the source, treat it as an assumption rather than as source-derived information.

## Faithfulness of means

For each means, examine whether:

* the source describes or supports the action, approach, capability, or resource;
* the model preserves the actor responsible for the means;
* the model preserves the intended object or recipient of the action;
* the means has been inferred through an unstated assumption;
* an existing fact in the source has been incorrectly interpreted as a means;
* a source-described means has been omitted or materially changed.

A means may be a reasonable inference even when it is not stated verbatim. In such cases, distinguish inference from direct source support.

## Faithfulness of influencers

For each influencer, examine whether:

* the alleged fact or condition is stated or supported by the source;
* the model preserves the relevant actor, circumstance, and context;
* uncertainty expressed in the source has been preserved;
* a possibility, prediction, recommendation, or desired state has been transformed into an established fact;
* an influencer has been introduced without source support;
* an important source-described influencer has been omitted.

Validation against a source establishes whether the influencer agrees with that source. It does not necessarily establish that the source itself is factually correct.

## Faithfulness of relationships

For each relationship, examine whether:

* the source explicitly states or reasonably supports the relationship;
* the direction of the relationship agrees with the source;
* the relationship preserves the relevant cause, contribution, quantification, influence, or dependency;
* the model introduces a relationship not supported by the source;
* a relationship stated in the source is missing from the model;
* an intermediate condition described by the source has been omitted;
* the relationship depends on an unstated assumption;
* combining multiple source statements has created a relationship that the source does not justify.

Do not assume that two elements should be related merely because both appear in the source. The source must support, imply, or permit the particular relationship represented in the model.

## Terminology and referential consistency

Examine whether:

* important source terms are represented consistently;
* synonyms or abbreviations in the model preserve the source meaning;
* the same actor, service, organisation, product, or system is referred to consistently;
* a model label introduces ambiguity not present in the source;
* pronouns or shortened names cause uncertainty about the intended referent;
* distinctions made in the source have been collapsed;
* one source concept has been divided into several model concepts without justification;
* several source concepts have been merged into one model concept without justification.

Differences in wording are not weaknesses by themselves. The relevant question is whether the meaning has been preserved.

## Assumptions

Examine whether:

* assumptions introduced by the modeller are stated explicitly;
* assumptions are clearly distinguishable from source-derived content;
* the model depends on assumptions that have not been disclosed;
* an assumption contradicts the source;
* an assumption changes the intended scope or meaning of the model;
* relationships based on assumptions can be identified.

Do not automatically treat every assumption as a weakness. Identify an assumption as problematic when it is hidden, contradictory, implausible in relation to the supplied material, or inconsistent with the modelling purpose.

## Explicit task requirements

If the source includes explicit modelling requirements, examine whether they have been satisfied. These may concern:

* required element types;
* minimum or maximum numbers of elements;
* required relationships;
* required classifications;
* required source items;
* required assumptions;
* required notation or labels;
* required model scope.

Distinguish failure to meet an explicit task requirement from a more general modelling weakness. Quote the relevant requirement when identifying non-compliance.

Do not invent requirements that are not contained in the supplied task or source.

## Alignment classifications

Use the following classifications when describing correspondence between source and model:

* **Aligned:** The model adequately represents the relevant source content.
* **Partially aligned:** Some meaning is preserved, but an important part is missing, altered, weakened, or expanded.
* **Missing from the model:** Relevant source content has no identifiable representation.
* **Unsupported addition:** Model content has no identifiable basis in the source and is not stated as an assumption.
* **Contradictory:** The model conflicts with the source.
* **Assumption:** The model extends the source through an explicitly or implicitly introduced supposition.
* **Cannot be determined:** The available material is insufficient for a reliable judgement.

Apply these classifications to particular elements, relationships, or source statements rather than assigning one classification to the complete model without explanation.

## Validation limits

Base the validation on the supplied model, source material, modelling purpose, and explicit task requirements.

Do not claim that:

* the source itself is factually correct;
* the model is suitable for purposes outside the stated scope;
* a missing source detail necessarily belongs in the model;
* an unsupported addition is false merely because it is absent from the source;
* an ambiguity can be resolved without evidence;
* internal BMM correctness has been established unless verification criteria have also been applied.

When the supplied material is insufficient for a definite judgement, state what cannot be determined and why.
